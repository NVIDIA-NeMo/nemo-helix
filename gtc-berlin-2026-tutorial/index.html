Fine-tune your own fast decision model on an open stack

Train a small open model to take over a decision that a large model makes inside your agent. Generate the training data, fine-tune on a single GPU and measure the result, all with open source tools in NeMo Helix.

What you're making

Meridian Compute is a made-up company that runs GPU clusters. Its incident agent runs on Nemotron 3 Super and works in steps:

  1. Read the alert, Slack message or ticket.
  2. route_incident picks which of 12 runbooks owns it and which of 4 actions Meridian's policy calls for. This is the step you fine-tune.
  3. Check the node's status.
  4. Page a team or open a ticket.

A general model doesn't know Meridian's policy. Even with the policy in its prompt, it picks the right action 70% of the time. The fine-tuned 4B model gets 82%.

The incident, a Slack message from the test set"dispatch queue on pod-c is lagging. jobs stuck pending, cli commands hanging or timing out after 30s. nothing is actually broken for users yet, but the scheduler controller seems to have stopped answering."

      Cook's notes

      • Use the packaged data for your first run. Generating it takes 21 minutes on a fast provider and about 14 hours on a free tier.
      • A deployment holds its GPU until you delete it. Do the clean-up step when you finish.
      • Every problem we hit is in KNOWN-ISSUES.md with its fix.

      How ours turned out

      Right action, on 416 test incidents none of the models trained onScore
      Qwen3.5-4B, untuned37.7%
      Nemotron 3 Ultra, no policy45.4%
      Nemotron 3 Ultra, policy in its prompt70.0%
      Qwen3.5-4B, fine-tuned in NeMo Helix82.0%

      Inside the running agent the fine-tuned model got 81% on 200 incidents, against 74% for Ultra with the policy. Measured on its own it answers in 301 ms against 670 ms, though a whole agent run takes about the same time either way. Full results in RESULTS.md

      Three ways to make it

      On a GPU machine

      Linux x86_64 with an NVIDIA GPU of 40 GB or more, CUDA 13 and the NVIDIA Container Toolkit. We used one A100 80GB. Do steps 0 to 5 on a laptop if you like.

      git clone -b gtc-berlin-2026-tutorial --single-branch https://github.com/NVIDIA-NeMo/nemo-helix.git helix-specialized-agent-tutorial
cd helix-specialized-agent-tutorial
bash scripts/preflight.sh

      With a coding agent

      Open the repo in Claude Code, Codex, Cursor or Pi. Its agent instructions and skill know what each step should print and which fix goes with which error. The agent asks before anything that spends money or holds a GPU.

      Walk me through the Helix specialized-agent tutorial, one step at a time.

      With your own ingredients

      Pick a step in your agent that runs often and has a fixed list of answers, where past examples teach more than documents do. Swap in your options and policy in taxonomy.json, your data and your agent's tool. Then rerun from step 4.
