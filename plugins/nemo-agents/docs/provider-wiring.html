How a secret reaches the service

NeMo stores the credential. A gateway-side driver resolves it. OpenShell injects it outside the agent’s sandbox.

Local storage or entity store?

Entity store is possible. Gateway location does not prevent an authenticated remote client.

Local SQLAlchemy + SQLite keeps replay checks and revocation metadata transactional without another remote request. It adds a persistent volume and single-host constraint.

A remote design needs equivalent atomic registration, restricted service access and explicit outage behavior.

Hard boundaries to keep in view
  • A placeholder is a lookup hint, not authorization. OpenShell must enforce sandbox-to-provider attachment.
  • The driver contract carries provider identity, not sandbox identity. Helix must create and restrict the right attachment.
  • Tickets last at most 5 minutes; bindings at most 1 hour. Resolution advertises a 30-second lifetime by default. That does not prove proxy eviction or refresh.
  • Deleting a binding stops future driver resolutions. It cannot erase a value already cached by OpenShell.
  • Secret permission changes need controller integration; the driver currently reads with its own service identity.
